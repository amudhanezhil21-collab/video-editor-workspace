Tab 2
If you invest in a Small Cap 250 Index Fund, you'll get 100% small-cap exposure.
Sounds logical, right?
After all, it's called a Small Cap 250 Index Fund.
But the reality is a little different.
The twist lies inside the index.
The Nifty Smallcap 250 Index is rebalanced only twice a year — in June and December.
Now, let's assume a small-cap company delivers an extraordinary rally.
Up 80%, 100%, or even 120%.
Its market capitalization increases and moves into the mid-cap range.
Does the index remove it immediately?
No.
That stock remains in the index until the next rebalancing.
And since an index fund tracks the index exactly, the fund continues to hold that stock as well.
That means you're officially holding a small-cap index fund...
But some of the stocks inside it may have already become mid-caps.
Take a look right now, before the June rebalancing.
Across all the funds tracking this index, more than 9% of the stocks have already crossed the mid-cap threshold.
That means roughly 9–10% of your "small-cap" fund exposure is in stocks that have technically become mid-caps.

Officially small-cap. Functionally, not entirely.
This structure prevents winners from being cut too early. A stock that has grown significantly continues to remain in the portfolio.
You may assume that all your money is taking pure small-cap risk.
Sometimes, that assumption can be inaccurate for a period of time.
That's why, along with looking at a fund's name, it's important to understand its underlying holdings as well.
