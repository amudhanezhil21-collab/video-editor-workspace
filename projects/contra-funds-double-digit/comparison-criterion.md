# The comparison criterion (creator directive)

**This governs every critic. Read it before judging anything.**

## What the comparison is NOT

It is **not** a blind beauty contest. Do not put two frames side by side and ask
"which is prettier". Do not judge motion graphics as decoration. Do not reward a
graphic for being elaborate, novel, or technically impressive on its own terms.

## What the comparison IS

Each video is trying to **say something**. The question is:

> **Which edit did the better job of exhibiting what *it* wanted to say?**

Judge our edit against the reference on *communicative effectiveness in the context
of its own content* — how well the craft is recruited to deliver the message.

The craft dimensions are the *means*, and each is judged by whether it served the
point being made at that moment:

- **motion graphics** — does the animation explain the idea, or just move?
- **text hierarchy** — does what matters most read first?
- **text placement & contrast** — is the eye taken to the right thing at the right time?
- **background** — does it support the point or compete with it?
- **colour theory** — does colour carry meaning (comparison, emphasis, category), or is it arbitrary?
- **design theory** — does structure mirror the logic of what's being said?
- **b-roll / AI b-roll** — does the cutaway earn its place by showing what words can't?
- **texture, feather, depth** — do they build focus, or noise?
- **sound design** — does audio mark meaning, or just fill space?

## How to compare fairly across two different videos

The two videos say **different things**. So never compare surface to surface.
Compare **by rhetorical function** — match the moment in ours to the moment in the
reference that is doing the *same job*, then ask which did that job better.

| Rhetorical function | Reference moment | Our moment |
|---|---|---|
| Hook / pose the question | opening | 0.05–5.08 "only 4 funds gave double digit… but why?" |
| Define the instrument | index explainer | 5.44–21.55 what a value fund is |
| Prove with data | the animated index table | 21.71–38.59 Nifty 500 Value 50 vs Nifty 50 / Nifty 500 |
| Explain a mechanism | rules-based methodology | 45.01–94.25 why each fund performed (DSP / LIC / Quant) |
| Land the caveat | risk framing | 103.04–108.61 risk taken to get those returns |
| Close / call to action | product card + endcard | 108.67–117.38 evaluate risk-adjusted performance |

For each row the critic asks: **did our treatment convey its point more clearly,
more vividly, and with better craft than the reference conveyed its equivalent point?**

## The verdict a critic must return

For each rhetorical function, one of:

- **OURS WINS** — ours conveys its point better than the reference conveys its equivalent
- **REFERENCE WINS** — the reference's equivalent moment communicates better, and *why*
- **TIE**

Plus, for every REFERENCE WINS or TIE: a **specific, actionable fix** naming what
to change — the graphic, the timing, the hierarchy, the placement — and what the
change should accomplish *communicatively*, not just aesthetically.

## Standing rules for critics

1. **Be harsh.** Default to finding fault. A generous verdict is a failed review.
2. **Never reward decoration.** A beautiful graphic that does not clarify the spoken
   point is a defect, not a win. Say so.
3. **Never punish restraint.** If a bare frame serves the moment better than a graphic
   would, that is correct, not lazy.
4. **Cite the spoken line.** Every finding names the timestamp and the words being
   said, and explains the gap between what is *said* and what is *shown*.
5. **Colour grading is out of scope** (creator directive). Never raise it.
6. **Judge our content on its own terms.** Ours is about *why four value funds
   outperformed*. Do not suggest we adopt the reference's subject matter, its
   location, its wardrobe, or its lighting — those are production facts we cannot
   change. Judge only what the edit controls.

## The loop

Fix → re-render → re-review. Continue until every rhetorical function returns
**OURS WINS** from a harsh critic. Do not stop early. Do not soften the bar to
finish.
