A comment asking for a **motion graphic** is a brief for a **visual**. It is never permission to put
the script's narration on the screen.

**Why:** on `flexi-cap-large-cap-disguise` (2026-08-25) the Sharpe-ratio and Sortino-ratio explainer
beats (ref21, ref23) had their `body` field filled with the script's own Hinglish sentences —
*"Har ek additional unit of risk ke badle fund ne kitna additional return diya. Jitna zyada Sharpe
Ratio, utne behtar risk-adjusted returns."* — set as a full-width paragraph. She flagged it:
*"unnecessarily the hinglish script from the script file is coming there, the comment was just about
to create a suitable motion graphics in it, not to put the Hinglish script on its screen."*

The direction for those beats read *"Full-screen Vox-style explainer of the Sharpe ratio… continuous
motion across the whole beat."* That is a request for a diagram that moves. Typesetting the sentence
is the laziest possible reading of it.

**Why it is wrong, not just plain:** she is saying those words while they sit on screen. The text
carries zero new information, doubles the reading load against her own voice, and pushes the actual
explanation — the thing a viewer needs a picture for — out of the frame. Captions are a separate
system with their own rules; a graphic is not a caption.

**How to apply:**
- **On-screen copy in a graphic is a LABEL**: the term, the number, the unit, a two-to-four-word tag.
  Not a sentence. Never a paragraph.
- **The visual carries the meaning.** For "Sharpe ratio" that is a risk axis against a return axis,
  one additional unit of risk yielding some additional unit of return, the ratio forming — labelled
  `Sharpe Ratio`, and nothing else. Then the words she speaks stay in the audio, where they belong.
- **Never Hinglish on screen.** The spoken track is Hinglish; on-screen copy is short English. Her own
  lower-thirds, table headers and topic cards are all English — that is the convention, and the
  script's language is not the frame's language.
- **The tell: a text field filled by copy-paste from the script or the transcript.** Copy for a frame
  is *authored* for that frame and derived from the concept. If a graphic's words could be pasted
  straight out of the narration, the graphic has not been designed yet.
- **What good looks like, same video:** ref26/ref28 lower-thirds carry two short authored English
  lines — *"Not large cap funds in disguise"* / *"They hold significantly more mid and small cap than
  the large cap funds of the same AMC."* Written for the frame, not lifted. That is the standard.
- Note it was the **script doc's** prose, not the spoken transcript (she paraphrases when recording).
  Either way it is narration, and narration is not frame copy.

**The general shape**, and it keeps recurring: reaching for the literal source instead of doing the
design work. Same as pasting a spreadsheet screenshot instead of building the table
([[feedback-tables-are-built-never-pasted]]) — the source material is an input to a graphic, never
the graphic itself.
